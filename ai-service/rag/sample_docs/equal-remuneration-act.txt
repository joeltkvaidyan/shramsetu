TITLE: Equal Remuneration Act, 1976
DEPARTMENT: Ministry of Labour and Employment, Government of India
LAST_UPDATED: 2025-01-10
SOURCE_URL: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192463
LAST_VERIFIED: 2026-10-01
STATUS: superseded
---
The Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 provides for equal remuneration to men and women workers for the same work or work of a similar nature. No employer shall pay remuneration at rates less favourable to women for the same work or work of similar nature done by men.

KEY PROVISIONS:
- Equal pay for equal work: Every employer shall pay equal remuneration to men and women for the same work or work of similar nature. No discrimination shall be made while recruiting or during employment.
- No discrimination in recruitment: No employer shall make any recruitment for the same work at lower rates of remuneration on the ground of sex.
- No discrimination during employment: No employer shall dismiss a woman worker or reduce her pay because she is pregnant or on maternity leave.
- Advisory Committee: The appropriate Government shall constitute an Advisory Committee to advise on increasing employment opportunities for women.
- Complaints: A woman worker or any recognised trade union can file a complaint with the Inspector if equal remuneration is not being paid.
- Penalty: Fine up to Rs 20,000 or imprisonment up to 3 months or both for non-compliance.

MIGRANT WOMEN WORKERS:
- Migrant women workers are entitled to equal pay for equal work regardless of their native state.
- Women working in agriculture, construction, and domestic work are covered under this Act.
- Employers cannot pay less because the woman worker is from a different state or belongs to a different community.

RELATED LAWS:
- The Equal Remuneration Act is being subsumed under the Code on Wages, 2019 which consolidates all four wage-related laws.
- The Code on Wages extends equal remuneration provisions to all workers regardless of gender.

REPEALED — HISTORICAL INFORMATION ONLY: The Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 was repealed when the Code on Wages, 2019 came into force on 21 November 2025. Everything above describes the law as it stood before that date; the prohibition of gender-based wage discrimination now continues under the Code on Wages, 2019. For the current law, see the "Code on Wages, 2019" document. Source: PIB press releases PRID 2192463 and PRID 2193095.
