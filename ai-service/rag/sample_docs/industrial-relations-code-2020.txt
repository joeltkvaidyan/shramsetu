The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 came into force on 21 November 2025 as one of the four
Labour Codes. It consolidated central laws on industrial relations — including the Trade
Unions Act, 1926, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 and the Industrial
Disputes Act, 1947 (complete list: needs review) — and it works alongside the Occupational
Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, which now covers the working conditions of
contract labour that were earlier regulated by the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition)
Act, 1970. (Source: PIB press release PRID 2192463.)

What the framework means for workers, including migrants:
- A worker employed through a contractor remains protected: the OSH Code, 2020 governs the
  licensing of contractors, wage payment responsibility and working conditions, while the
  Industrial Relations Code governs disputes and employment relations.
- Workers continue to have the right to raise industrial disputes through the grievance
  redressal and conciliation machinery set up under the Code.
- Fixed-term employees are entitled to benefits equivalent to permanent employees, on a
  proportionate basis.

If your rights are violated:
- File a complaint with the Inspector-cum-Facilitator of the area where the establishment is
  located.
- Approach the Labour Court or Industrial Tribunal for disputes under the Code.
- Call the labour helpline for guidance, or visit the nearest Common Service Centre.

Note: threshold numbers of employees for certain provisions under the Code are specific and
have been amended by notification — check the current text on labour.gov.in (needs review for
exact thresholds).
