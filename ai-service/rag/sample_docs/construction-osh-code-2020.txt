Construction workers, including migrant construction workers, are now governed by the
Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (OSH Code), which came into force
on 21 November 2025. The OSH Code replaced the earlier Building and Other Construction Workers
(Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1996 and the Building and Other
Construction Workers' Welfare Cess Act, 1996, among the 13 central labour laws it consolidated.
(Source: PIB press release PRID 2192463.)

What continues for construction workers under the new framework:
- State Building and Other Construction Workers' Welfare Boards continue to operate, funded by
  a cess on construction cost, and provide welfare benefits to registered construction workers:
  medical assistance, maternity benefit for women workers, education assistance for children,
  pension, assistance for tools, accident and death benefits. (Exact scheme list varies by state
  — needs review for your state.)
- Registration: a construction worker who has completed the prescribed number of working days
  in the preceding year can register with the Welfare Board of the state where they are working.
  Registration is not restricted to the worker's home state, so a migrant construction worker
  can register where they currently work.
- Employer obligations: safety measures at the worksite, payment of the welfare cess, and
  registration of covered establishments continue under the Code.

How to register and claim:
- Approach the Construction Workers' Welfare Board office (formerly BOCW Board) in the district
  where you work, with proof of construction work, age proof and address proof, and bank account
  details.
- After registration you receive a welfare board card that can be used to avail benefits.
- For safety violations at the worksite, file a complaint with the Inspector-cum-Facilitator
  under the OSH Code, or call the labour helpline.
