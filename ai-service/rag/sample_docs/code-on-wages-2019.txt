The Code on Wages, 2019 (No. 29 of 2019) is now the single central law governing wages and bonus
for all workers in India, including migrant workers. It came into force on 21 November 2025,
together with the other three Labour Codes, and it replaced four earlier wage laws: the Payment
of Wages Act, 1936, the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 and the Equal
Remuneration Act, 1976. (Sources: PIB press release PRID 2193095; the official text of the Code
is published on labour.gov.in.)

What the Code gives every worker, including migrants:
- Minimum wages: The appropriate Government (Central or State) fixes minimum wages that every
  employer must pay. The Code introduced a national floor wage, below which no State can fix
  its minimum wages. Workers doing the same or similar work must receive the same wages
  regardless of gender.
- Timely payment: Wages must be paid within the wage period as prescribed under the Code, and
  deductions that are not authorised by law are prohibited.
- Equal remuneration: An employer cannot discriminate between men and women in wages for the
  same or similar work.
- Bonus: Eligible workers continue to receive statutory bonus as consolidated under the Code.
- Universal coverage: Unlike the earlier Minimum Wages Act, the Code covers all employees and
  all employments, organised and unorganised alike.

A uniform definition of wages (basic pay plus dearness allowance plus retaining allowance)
applies across the Labour Codes, which is designed to ensure higher social security benefits
such as gratuity and maternity benefit. (Source: PIB Year End Review 2025, PRID 2209767.)

If you are not paid your wages:
- File a complaint with the Inspector-cum-Facilitator of the area where you work.
- The Code provides a claims mechanism for recovery of unpaid wages; a worker can approach the
  authority designated under the Code.
- Call the labour helpline or the e-Shram helpline 14434 for guidance, or visit the nearest
  Common Service Centre.

Note: exact minimum wage amounts differ by state and employment and are revised periodically.
