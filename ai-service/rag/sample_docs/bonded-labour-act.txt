The Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976 abolishes the bonded labour system and provides for the liberation and rehabilitation of bonded labourers. Bonded labour is a form of forced labour where a person works to repay a debt under conditions that are exploitative.

KEY PROVISIONS:
- Abolition: The bonded labour system is abolished. Every bond or agreement giving rise to the bonded labour system is void.
- Definition of bonded labour: A person who has entered into a bond under the bondage system to render work or service or payment of debt or for a period of service for a debt.
- Prohibition: No person shall make any advance under the bondage system, or compel any person to enter into any bond, or accept any forced labour.
- Penalties: Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to Rs 2,000 or both for whoever compels a person to enter into a bond or advances money under the bondage system.
- State vigilance committees: Each State shall constitute a Vigilance Committee to monitor abolition of bonded labour.
- District Magistrate: Has power to identify bonded labourers, order their release, and ensure rehabilitation.

REHABILITATION:
- Released bonded labourers are entitled to rehabilitation assistance.
- Rehabilitation includes: land grant, housing, skill training, education for children, and financial assistance.
- National Fund for Bonded Labour provides resources for rehabilitation.
- State Governments have created State-level funds for rehabilitation.

MIGRANT WORKERS:
- Migrant workers are particularly vulnerable to bonded labour, especially in brick kilns, agriculture, mining, and construction.
- Interstate migrant workers who are bonded labourers can be identified and rescued by the District Magistrate.
- Rehabilitation includes skill development under PMKVY (Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana).
- Migrant bonded labourers have the right to return to their native state with assistance.

COMPLAINTS:
- Any person can file a complaint with the District Magistrate or the Sub-Divisional Magistrate.
- Complaints are treated as cognizable offences.
- Childline 1098 can be contacted to report bonded child labour.
