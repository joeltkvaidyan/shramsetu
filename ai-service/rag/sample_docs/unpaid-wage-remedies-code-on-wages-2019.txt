TITLE: Unpaid Wages — How to Recover Them: Complaints, Claims and Time Limits under the Code on Wages, 2019

If your employer does not pay your wages, or pays less than the agreed or
notified amount, the law gives you a route to get the money back. This document
sets out that route under the Code on Wages, 2019, which came into force on
21 November 2025 and replaced the Payment of Wages Act, 1936, the Minimum
Wages Act, 1948, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 and the Equal Remuneration
Act, 1976. (Source: PIB press release PRID 2193095.)

WHAT COUNTS AS UNPAID WAGES
- "Wages" means basic pay plus dearness allowance plus retaining allowance, and
  includes the payments the Code treats as wages.
- Withholding wages, delaying payment beyond the due date, paying less than
  the agreed rate, and making deductions that are not authorised by law are
  all violations. An employer cannot pay wages in kind (goods instead of cash)
  without your agreement, and cannot withhold your wages as security.
- Migrant workers have exactly the same rights here as other workers. Working
  in a different state from where you were recruited does not remove them.

STEP 1 — ASK THE EMPLOYER, AND KEEP THE RECORD
- Ask your employer or the contractor/agent who engaged you for the amount and
  the date the wages were due.
- Keep proof of the work you did and the wages owed: wage slips, a written
  contract or appointment letter, attendance or work-site registers, bank
  statements showing no credit, and photographs of you at the work site.
- Note the name of the establishment, its address, the site where you worked
  and the person who engaged you. Contract labour usually means the principal
  employer and the contractor are both answerable.

STEP 2 — COMPLAINT TO THE INSPECTOR-CUM-FACILITATOR
- The Code provides for an Inspector-cum-Facilitator. You can make a complaint
  about unpaid wages to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator for the area where you
  actually worked.
- Where you worked matters: the complaint goes to the authority for the area
  of your work, not the area where the employer's office is registered.
- A complaint is made in person or through the prescribed complaint channel of
  the State Labour Department. Many States also accept it through the labour
  department's online complaint portal or the Common Service Centre (CSC).

STEP 3 — CLAIM APPLICATION TO THE DESIGNATED AUTHORITY
- If the complaint does not settle the matter, or the amount is disputed, you
  can make a claim application to the authority designated under the Code.
- A designated authority holds an inquiry and passes an award for the amount
  found due. Recovery of the awarded amount is then enforced against the
  employer, and the award carries the costs of the proceedings.
- A claim application is a separate step from the complaint: the complaint is
  how the violation is reported, the claim application is how the money is
  ordered. Taking a complaint to the inspector does not automatically recover
  the amount for you — you still need the claim to be decided.

TIME LIMITS — CHECK BEFORE YOU WAIT
- The Code carries forward the time limits that applied under the Payment of
  Wages Act, 1936: a complaint is normally to be made within three years of
  the date the wages became due, and a claim application within six years.
- These limits are the reason to act early rather than keep waiting. Confirm
  the limits in force and the correct forum for your State before relying on
  them — a complaint filed after the limit can be rejected without reaching
  the merits. (State-specific procedure and limitation periods need review at
  the time of answering.)

IF YOU ARE A MIGRANT WORKER
- Register on the e-Shram portal (eshram.gov.in) and keep your UAN: it is the
  single record of your work history and is useful when claiming wages.
- You can approach a Common Service Centre (CSC) for help filing a complaint.
- The labour helpline is 14434.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT
- It cannot tell you the amount your employer actually owes you, or which
  authority has jurisdiction in your district, or the exact form and fee for
  your State. Those depend on the facts and on the State rules.
- Wage recovery in practice needs the Inspector-cum-Facilitator or a
  designated authority, not an online form alone. Use this as a guide to what
  steps exist, then confirm the local procedure with the nearest labour
  office or CSC.
