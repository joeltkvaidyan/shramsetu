TITLE: Minimum Wages — Legal Protections for All Workers Including Migrants
DEPARTMENT: Ministry of Labour and Employment, Government of India
LAST_UPDATED: 2025-01-30
---
The Minimum Wages Act, 1948 (now subsumed under the Code on Wages, 2019) ensures that every 
worker, including migrant workers, receives at least the minimum wage fixed by the government 
for their category of work. This applies to all occupations and is enforceable across India.

Key provisions:
- Floor Wage: The Central Government fixes a national floor wage below which no worker can be 
  paid. As of 2024, the national floor wage is Rs 178 per day.
- State Minimum Wages: Each state fixes its own minimum wages for different categories of 
  workers (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled, highly skilled) and for different scheduled 
  employments. These are typically higher than the floor wage.
- Equal pay: Workers doing the same work must receive the same wages, regardless of whether 
  they are migrants or locals. Gender-based wage discrimination is prohibited.
- Payment timing: Wages must be paid on the last working day of the wage period (weekly for 
  workers earning up to Rs 18,000/month, and fortnightly or monthly for others).
- Overtime: Workers must be paid at least twice the ordinary rate of wages for overtime work 
  (beyond 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week).

Rights of migrant workers regarding wages:
- A migrant worker is entitled to the same minimum wage as a local worker doing the same type 
  of work in the same state.
- The contractor or employer cannot pay less than the state minimum wage, even if the worker 
  has come from another state.
- If a worker is not being paid minimum wages, they can file a complaint with the Labour 
  Commissioner or the District Magistrate of the area where they are working.
- Workers can also call the toll-free labour helpline 14400 for assistance.

How to check minimum wages:
- Visit the website of the state Labour Department where you are working.
- The Code on Wages portal (https://codonwages.gov.in) provides information about minimum 
  wages across states.
- The worker's payslip should show the gross wage, deductions, and net wage paid.

Recovery of wages: If wages are not paid, a worker can:
- File a complaint with the Inspector-cum-Facilitator under the Code on Wages.
- Approach the Labour Court or Industrial Tribunal.
- The claim can be filed within one year from the date the wages became due.
