TITLE: Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 — Protections for Contract Workers
DEPARTMENT: Ministry of Labour and Employment, Government of India
LAST_UPDATED: 2024-12-10
---
The Contract Labour Act regulates the employment of contract labour and provides protections 
to workers hired through contractors. Many migrant workers are employed as contract labourers 
in factories, construction sites, and other establishments.

Applicability:
- Applies to every establishment where 10 or more contract labourers are employed (or have 
  been employed on any day in the preceding 12 months).
- Applies to every contractor who employs 10 or more contract labourers.
- Does not apply to workers in ports, docks, or certain specified establishments.

Key protections:
- Registration: The principal employer must register the establishment with the Labour 
  Commissioner. The contractor must obtain a licence.
- Wages: Contract workers must be paid at least the minimum wages applicable in the state. 
  The principal employer is jointly liable for wage payments if the contractor fails to pay.
- Working conditions: Adequate rest rooms, drinking water, toilets, and first-aid facilities 
  must be provided at the workplace.
- Equal treatment: Contract workers should not be treated differently from regular employees 
  doing similar work in terms of wages and working conditions.
- No exploitation: The contractor cannot charge any fee from the worker for providing 
  employment. The worker's wages must be paid directly or through bank transfer.

Rights of contract workers:
- Right to receive wages on time (within the wage period).
- Right to work in safe conditions with protective clothing and safety equipment.
- Right to approach the Labour Commissioner if rights are violated.
- Right to be registered under EPF/ESI if applicable.

How to file a complaint:
- Approach the Inspector-cum-Facilitator of the area where the establishment is located.
- File a written complaint with details of the violation.
- The inspector can impose penalties on the contractor and/or principal employer.
- Call the labour helpline 14400 for guidance.
