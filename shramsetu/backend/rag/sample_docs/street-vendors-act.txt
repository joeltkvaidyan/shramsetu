TITLE: Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014
DEPARTMENT: Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, Government of India
LAST_UPDATED: 2025-02-01
---
The Street Vendors Act, 2014 protects the rights of street vendors and provides for regulation of street vending. It ensures that no street vendor can be evicted without due process and provides for identification, registration, and licensing.

KEY PROVISIONS:
- Definition: Street vendor means a person engaged in vending of articles, goods, wares, food items or merchandise of everyday use to the public in a public place or a private area adjacent to a public place.
- Town Vending Committee (TVC): Every local authority shall constitute a TVC consisting of representatives of street vendors, local authority, police, and other stakeholders.
- Certificate of vending: Every street vendor shall be issued a certificate of vending by the TVC after verification.
- vending certificate validity: The certificate shall be valid for a period of 5 years and may be renewed.
- No eviction without due process: No street vendor shall be evicted or their goods confiscated without following the procedure laid down in the Act.
- Relocation: If vending is to be discontinued due to development projects, the vendor shall be relocated to an alternative site.
- Penalty: Street vending without a certificate can attract a fine of up to Rs 2,000.
- Rehabilitation: Vendors displaced due to development projects are entitled to rehabilitation.

PM-SVANIDHI (PRADHAN MANTRI STREET VENDORS ATMA NIDHI NIDHI):
- Special credit scheme for street vendors affected by COVID-19.
- Collateral-free working capital loans up to Rs 10,000 for the first tranche.
- Second tranche: Up to Rs 20,000 after repaying the first tranche on time.
- Third tranche: Up to Rs 50,000 after repaying the second tranche on time.
- 7% annual interest subsidy on timely repayment (credited directly to the vendor's bank account).

MIGRANT STREET VENDORS:
- Migrant street vendors can register and obtain vending certificate in the city where they are vending.
- They need not be permanent residents of the city where they vend.
- The TVC cannot deny registration based on the vendor's native state.
- Migrant vendors are equally eligible for PM-SVANidhi loans.
